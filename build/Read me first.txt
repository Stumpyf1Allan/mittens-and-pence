Mittens & Pence
=======

What it is
----------
A small app for keeping track of your bank accounts, budgets and investments.
It runs entirely on your own computer.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. There is no sign-in, no account and no password.
Your figures live in one file on your own disk and go nowhere else. The only
things it ever reaches out to are the price feed (to value shares) and, if you
choose to link one, your own bank.


Running it — Windows
--------------------
1. Double-click Mittens & Pence.exe.

2. The first time, Windows will say "Windows protected your PC".
   Click "More info", then "Run anyway".

   This is normal and expected. It appears because the app is not
   code-signed — a certificate costs a few hundred pounds a year, which is
   hard to justify for an app shared between a few people. Windows shows the
   same warning for any unsigned app.

3. Give it ten to twenty seconds the first time. It unpacks itself, then a
   window opens. Later starts are quicker.

If your antivirus quarantines it, that is the same cause: unsigned and
self-unpacking. Allow it, or ask whoever sent it to you.


Running it — Mac
----------------
There are two Mac files, and you need the right one. A Mac built since late
2020 has an Apple chip; an older one has an Intel chip, and the two need
different files. Pick the wrong one and it simply won't open.

  Apple menu (top left) > About This Mac.
    "Chip: Apple M1" (or M2, M3, M4...)  ->  the -arm64 file
    "Processor: ... Intel ..."           ->  the -x86_64 file

Then:

1. Double-click the .zip. Mittens & Pence appears beside it. Drag it into
   your Applications folder — the app works from anywhere, but Applications
   is where you'll find it again.

2. Double-click it. macOS will refuse, and say it can't be opened because
   Apple can't check it for malicious software. That is expected. Click Done.

   It is the same cause as the Windows warning above — the app isn't
   code-signed — and macOS shows it for any unsigned app.

3. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, and scroll down. There is
   a line about Mittens & Pence being blocked, with an "Open Anyway" button
   beside it. Click that, then Open on the confirmation.

   You only do this once. After that it opens like anything else.

   (Older Macs let you right-click the app and choose Open instead. Newer
   ones don't, so the route above is the one that always works.)


First time you open it
----------------------
It walks you round the tabs once and asks what to call you. Then either:

  * "Add my first institution" — pick your bank or broker from the list, and
    it tells you honestly whether it can link automatically or whether you
    need to download a statement.

  * "Have a look with sample data first" — a made-up household, so you can
    click around before entering anything real. Settings clears it later.


The monthly job, and the folder that does it
--------------------------------------------
Most banks can't be linked for free, so once a month you download last month's
statement for each of those accounts. Mittens & Pence makes you a folder for
them — the tour shows you where it is, and Settings has a button to open it.

  1. Download the statements. Any format the bank offers is fine.
  2. Drop the files straight into the folder. Don't rename them: the bank's
     own filename usually names the account, and that is how the app knows
     where the transactions belong.
  3. Open Mittens & Pence and press "Sync everything".

That's it. The files are read, added, and moved into a "Filed" folder so you
can see what has already gone in. Nothing is ever deleted.

If a file can't be matched to an account with confidence, it is LEFT where it
is and the app tells you, rather than guessing and putting a year of spending
in the wrong place. Import that one by hand once and it will recognise the next
one on its own.

Receipts can go in the folder too. They are sorted by month and kept so you can
find one later. They are not read — no figures are taken off a photograph.

Importing the same file twice is safe: every transaction is fingerprinted, so a
row that is already in cannot go in again.


The monthly reminder
--------------------
During the tour you can give an email address, and in Settings you choose which
day of the month to be reminded (29, 30 and 31 all mean the last day, which is
28 in February and 29 in a leap year).

Mittens & Pence runs on your own computer and has no mail server behind it, so
it cannot post you an email out of the blue — there would be nothing running to
send it. Instead it gives you a calendar entry: Settings > "Add it to my
calendar" saves a small file, you open it once, and your own calendar repeats
the reminder every month and alerts you wherever you are, phone included. If
your calendar is set up to email you about events, you get an email.

There is also a strip across the top of the app on and after the day, until
you've actually imported something that month.


How your spending gets sorted
-----------------------------
Every payment lands in a category on its own, using about 200 built-in rules
(Tesco, Shell, Octopus, Netflix and so on). Anything it gets wrong takes one
click to fix, and it offers to remember the correction and apply it to
everything else that matches.

The one that catches people out is Shopping against Food & Drink. The line is
things you CONSUME against things you BUY AND KEEP — and it is decided by the
shop, not by what was in the basket:

  Food & Drink   Groceries (the supermarket shop), Restaurants & takeaway,
                 Coffee & snacks, Alcohol

  Shopping       Clothing, Electronics, Household & general, Hobbies,
                 Books & media, Gifts given

So one Tesco payment counts as Groceries in full, even if a third of it was a
frying pan and a birthday card. Your bank sends one amount and has no idea what
was in the trolley.

When a shop like that matters, SPLIT the payment: open it in Budget >
Transactions, click Split, and divide it across as many categories as you like.
The parts have to add up to the penny, and the original is left exactly as your
bank sent it — so re-uploading the statement is still safe.

"Household & general" is for the shop that sells everything: Amazon, eBay,
John Lewis, Argos, Wilko, Poundland. There is no sensible way to guess whether
an Amazon order was a kettle or a pack of nappies, so it does not try.


Where your data is kept
-----------------------
Windows:  C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Local\Mittens and Pence\
Mac:      ~/Library/Application Support/Mittens and Pence/

Settings tells you the exact folder and has a button to open it. Everything is
in there: one database file plus your exported spreadsheets. Back that folder
up and you have backed up everything. Delete it and the app starts empty.


New versions
------------
If the person who sent you this publishes a fix, your copy notices on its own,
usually within a day, and downloads it in the background. A strip appears at
the top of the window when it is ready, with a button that shows you the file.
Close the app and run the new one — on a Mac, unzip it first and drop the app
into Applications, replacing the old one. Everything you have entered stays
exactly where it is — your data lives in the folder above, and every version reads it.

It will never replace itself while you are using it, and it never installs
anything without you. Settings > Updates turns the whole thing off if you would
rather look for new versions yourself.


Reading it on your phone
------------------------
There is no phone app, but there is something better than nothing:
Export a Spreadsheet > "A copy for your phone" makes a single file with your
headline figures laid out for a small screen. Put it in iCloud, Google Drive or
Dropbox, or email it to yourself, and open it on your phone. It works with no
signal and sends nothing anywhere. It is a snapshot of the moment you made it,
not a live feed, so make a fresh one when you want fresher numbers.


Stuck?
------
There is a cat button in the bottom-right corner of every screen. Click it and
it opens an email to whoever looks after the app, with the version and which
screen you were on already filled in — and nothing at all about your money.
Say what happened and send it.
